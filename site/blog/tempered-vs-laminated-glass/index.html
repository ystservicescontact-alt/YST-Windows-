Tempered vs. laminated glass: what your windows and doors need

The difference between regular, tempered and laminated glass, where each is required, and which to choose for security, safety and noise.

When a window or door glass breaks, one of the first questions is what kind of glass it needs. The answer affects safety, security and sometimes what the building code requires.

Regular (annealed) glass

Standard window glass. It's the least expensive, but it breaks into large, sharp pieces. It's fine for most ordinary windows that aren't near floors, doors or bathtubs.

Tempered glass

Tempered glass is heat-treated to be about four times stronger than regular glass. When it does break, it shatters into small, blunt pieces that are much less likely to cause injury.

  • Usually required in doors, sidelites and patio doors
  • Usually required in glass close to the floor, near stairs and around bathtubs and showers
  • Can't be cut after it's made, so it's always ordered to the exact size

Laminated glass

Laminated glass is two layers of glass bonded with a clear plastic interlayer. When it breaks, the pieces stick to the interlayer and the glass stays in the frame.

  • Security: much harder to break through, so it slows down break-ins
  • Noise: reduces street and traffic noise
  • UV: blocks most UV light, which protects floors and furniture from fading
  • Used in skylights and storefronts, and a good choice for ground-floor and basement windows

Which do you need?

For a broken door or a window near the floor, usually tempered. For security after a break-in, laminated. For a street-facing bedroom, laminated helps with noise. We check what's required and recommend the right glass when we quote. See window glass replacement.
