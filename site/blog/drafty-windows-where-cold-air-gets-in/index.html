Drafty windows: where the cold air gets in and how to stop it

Find exactly where cold air leaks into your home around the windows, and the right fix for each spot, from weatherstripping to new glass.

A drafty window doesn't always need replacing. Cold air usually comes in at one or two specific spots, and each one has its own fix. Here's how to find them.

How to find a draft

On a windy day, hold a lit candle or a strip of tissue paper near the window and move it slowly around the edges. Where it flickers or moves, air is getting in. A cheap infrared thermometer also works: cold spots show where air or heat is leaking.

1. Between the sash and the frame

The most common spot. Weatherstripping flattens, tears or falls off over time. New weatherstripping restores the seal. On casement windows, also check that the lock pulls the sash tight. See window lock repair.

2. Around the outside of the frame

Cracked or missing exterior caulking lets wind and water in between the frame and the wall. The old caulking needs to be cut out and replaced, not covered over. See window caulking.

3. Through the glass

A single pane or a double pane unit that has lost its seal and insulating gas feels cold to the touch. It isn't a draft, but it makes the room feel drafty. A new sealed unit with Low-E glass fixes it. See foggy window repair.

4. A window that doesn't close all the way

A bent hinge or a worn crank can leave a casement open a few millimetres. You may not see it, but you'll feel it. See hinge repair.

5. Around the interior trim

Gaps between the frame and the drywall can let cold air in from the wall cavity. Removing the trim and filling the gap with low-expansion foam fixes it.

Fix drafts before winter. Exterior caulking needs dry weather above freezing to cure properly. Read our winter window checklist.
