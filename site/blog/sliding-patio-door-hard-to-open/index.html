Sliding patio door hard to open? 5 causes and how to fix them

Why sliding patio doors drag, grind or jump the track, what you can fix yourself, and when the rollers need replacing.

A sliding patio door should glide with one hand. If you have to pull with your whole body, something is wrong, and forcing it makes it worse. Here are the usual causes, from easiest to hardest.

1. A dirty track

Sand, grit and leaves collect in the bottom track and jam the rollers. Vacuum the track, then wipe it with a damp cloth. Don't use oil or grease: they collect more dirt.

2. Rollers set too low

Most doors have adjustment screws at the bottom corners of the moving panel, often behind a small plastic cap. If the door drags on the track, the rollers may have dropped. Raising them a little can make a big difference.

3. Worn or broken rollers

This is the most common cause. The small wheels under the door flatten, crack or seize after years of use. You'll hear grinding or feel the door bump along. The fix is new rollers: the panel is lifted out, the old roller assemblies are replaced, and the door is set back and adjusted.

4. A damaged track

If the raised rail the rollers ride on is bent or worn flat, new rollers won't glide properly. A damaged rail can often be repaired or covered with a track cap rather than replacing the door.

5. The frame has moved

If the house has settled, the frame can go out of square, so the door rubs or won't lock. Adjusting the rollers and the lock keeper usually brings it back.

When to call

If cleaning the track doesn't help, or the door jumps off the track, call us before it gets worse. Patio door panels are heavy and the glass is large, so lifting them out is a two-person job. See patio door repair.
