Why is there condensation between my window panes?

Fog or moisture between double pane glass means the seal has failed. What causes it, whether it can be fixed, and what it costs compared with a new window.

If there's fog, droplets or a milky haze between the two panes of your window, and you can't wipe it off from either side, the window's seal has failed. It's one of the most common window problems in the GTA, and the fix is simpler than most people expect.

How a double pane window works

A double pane window is a sealed unit: two sheets of glass held apart by a spacer bar around the edge. The space between them is sealed and often filled with argon gas. The spacer contains a drying agent that absorbs any moisture trapped during manufacturing.

Why the seal fails

  • Age: seals usually last 15 to 25 years.
  • Temperature swings: Toronto's freeze and thaw cycles make the glass expand and contract, working the seal loose.
  • Sun: south and west facing windows age faster.
  • Water sitting on the seal: blocked drainage at the bottom of the frame keeps the edge of the glass wet.

Once the seal fails, humid air gets in, the drying agent fills up, and moisture condenses on the inside of the glass.

Is it a problem?

Beyond the view, a failed unit has lost its insulating gas, so the window lets more heat out in winter. Over time the moisture leaves permanent mineral stains on the glass.

Can it be fixed?

Yes. The frame is almost always fine. The fix is to replace just the sealed glass unit: we measure it, have a new unit made to size, and install it in your existing frame. Your trim and walls aren't touched. See foggy window repair.

What about defogging?

Some services drill small holes, clean the glass and seal the holes. It can look clearer for a while, but the seal is still broken and the insulating gas is gone. Replacing the unit is the lasting fix.

Condensation on the inside of the glass is different

If the water is on the room side and you can wipe it off, the seal is fine. That's indoor humidity, and you can reduce it with fans and a lower humidifier setting. Read our winter window checklist.
