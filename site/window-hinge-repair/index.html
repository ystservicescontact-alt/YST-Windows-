Signs your hinges need replacing

  • The window rubs on the frame or has to be pushed to close
  • The sash sags or hangs lower on one side
  • You see rust, bent arms or a broken rivet at the top or bottom of the sash
  • The window swings in the wind when it's open

Why hinges fail

Wind catching an open window is the most common cause: it bends the arms. Rust, dirt in the track and years of use wear them out too. A bent hinge also strains the crank operator and the glass, so it's worth fixing early.

What we do

We remove the sash, replace both hinges with the right length and type, reinstall the sash and adjust it so it closes and locks evenly. We check the crank operator while we're there.

Types of window hinges

  • Casement hinges (four-bar hinges) on the top and bottom of side-opening windows. They let the sash swing out and slide along a track.
  • Awning hinges on the sides of windows that open from the bottom.
  • Friction stays on many condo and European windows, including FAPIM, that hold the vent open by friction.

Hinges come in different lengths and track styles, so we match the size, the hole pattern and the material. Stainless steel hinges last longest, especially on windows that face the weather.

Hinge or operator?

If the window drags on the frame, won't close square or the corner drops, it's usually the hinges. If the crank turns but the window doesn't move, it's usually the operator. See window crank repair. Often both are worn, and we fix them in one visit.

Common questions

Should both hinges be replaced?

Usually yes. The top and bottom hinges wear together, and replacing both keeps the sash square.

Why does the bottom corner of my casement window drag?

Worn or bent hinges let the sash drop, so the corner rubs the frame. New hinges lift it back into place.

How do you know which hinge size I need?

We measure the hinge track and check the brand and hole pattern. A photo of the open window with the hinge visible helps us quote.
