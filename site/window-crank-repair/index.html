Hardware we repair and replace

  • Crank operators that spin without moving the window (stripped gears)
  • Crank handles that are loose, cracked or missing
  • Hinges (the long arms at the top and bottom of a casement) that are bent, seized or sagging
  • Multi-point locks and lock handles that won't pull the window tight
  • Tilt latches and balances on single and double hung windows that won't stay up
  • Awning window operators and arms

Why it matters

A casement that doesn't close tight lets in cold air and water, and a window that won't lock is a security problem. A bent hinge can also put stress on the glass and the frame. Replacing the hardware early prevents bigger repairs later.

Matching parts

Window hardware varies by brand and year. We identify your operator and hinges and bring matching or compatible parts. Sending a photo of the crank and hinge ahead of time often lets us fix it in a single visit.

What affects the price

Every window is different. The price depends on:

  • Which parts need replacing: operator, handle, hinges or locks
  • How many windows need work
  • Brand and age of the hardware
  • Whether parts are standard or need to be special-ordered

Hardware we replace

Crank operators

The gearbox that opens and closes casement and awning windows.

Folding handles

The handle you turn to operate the window.

Hinges

The metal arms at the top and bottom that let the sash swing open.

Multi-point locks

Locks that pull the sash tight at several points along the frame.

Keepers and strike plates

The parts on the frame that the locks hook into.

Tilt latches and balances

The parts that let hung windows tilt in and stay open.

Hardware brands we work with

Most Canadian casement and awning windows use hardware from a handful of makers. We find matching or compatible parts for:

  • AmesburyTruth (formerly Truth Hardware), the most common in Canadian casements
  • Roto, common on European-style and tilt-and-turn windows
  • Maxim, found on many older vinyl windows
  • Encore, common in builder-grade homes
  • Entrygard and other operator systems

Not sure what you have? Text us a close-up photo of your crank and the hinge arm and we'll identify it.

Why cranks fail

  • Freeze and thaw cycles cause rust and corrosion in the gears
  • Lack of lubrication lets dirt seize the mechanism
  • Forcing a window that's frozen shut strips the gears
  • Normal wear after 15 to 20 years of daily use

Make your hardware last longer

  1. Lubricate once a year with a silicone spray on hinges and moving parts.
  2. Keep tracks clean. Vacuum dirt out of the hinge tracks each spring.
  3. Check weatherstripping. Worn strips make the window harder to close and strain the lock.
  4. Never force it. If a window won't close in winter, let it thaw or call us.

Common questions

My crank turns but the window doesn't move. What's wrong?

The gears inside the operator are stripped. The fix is a new crank operator, not a new window.

Can you fix a window that won't close all the way?

Usually yes. It's often a bent hinge, a worn operator or a lock that needs adjusting.

Do you carry parts for older windows?

We find matching or compatible parts for most windows, including older and discontinued models.
