Foggy window repair in Toronto and the GTA

Why windows fog up between the panes

A double or triple pane window is a sealed unit: two or three sheets of glass held apart by a spacer bar, sealed around the edge, and often filled with argon gas. The spacer holds a drying agent that soaks up any moisture trapped during manufacturing.

After years of Toronto freeze and thaw cycles, sun and wind, that edge seal breaks down. Humid air gets in, the drying agent fills up, and moisture condenses on the inside surfaces of the glass. That's the fog, haze or white film you see.

Signs your seal has failed

  • Fog, droplets or a milky haze between the panes that won't wipe off from either side
  • White mineral streaks or spots inside the glass, even on dry days
  • The fog comes and goes with the weather or time of day
  • The room near the window feels colder than it used to

Why we replace the glass, not the window

When the frame is in good shape, there's no reason to tear it out. We measure the existing sealed unit, order a new one made to your exact size, and install it in the same frame. You keep your frame, trim, paint and drywall untouched, and it usually costs far less than a new window.

New units can be upgraded while we're at it: Low-E coating to keep heat in during winter, argon fill, warm-edge spacers that reduce condensation at the edges, or triple pane where the frame allows it.

What about "defogging" services?

Some companies drill the glass and spray in a cleaning solution. It can make the glass look clearer for a while, but it doesn't restore the seal or the insulating gas. We replace the unit so the window insulates properly again.

Condensation on the room side of the glass in winter is a different problem. It's usually high indoor humidity, not a broken seal. If you can wipe it off, the seal is fine, and we'll tell you so.

What affects the price

Every window is different. The price depends on:

  • The size of each glass unit
  • Glass thickness and the gap between the panes
  • Glass type: clear, Low-E, frosted, tinted or tempered
  • Shape: rectangle, arch or custom
  • How many windows you're fixing
  • Access: ground floor or upper floors

Upgrade while you're at it

A new sealed unit doesn't have to match the old one exactly. Since the glass is being made anyway, you can choose:

  • Low-E coating to reflect heat back into the room in winter and keep summer sun out
  • Argon gas fill for better insulation than plain air
  • Warm-edge spacers that reduce condensation and frost at the edge of the glass
  • Triple pane where the frame is deep enough, for quieter, warmer rooms

Why repair beats replacement

Replacing a whole window means removing interior trim, patching drywall and repainting. When only the sealed unit has failed, none of that is needed. The frame, sash and hardware are usually still in good shape. If the frame is too damaged to save, we'll tell you so.

Common questions

Can you fix a foggy window without replacing the whole window?

Yes. In most cases we replace only the sealed glass unit and keep your existing frame and sash.

How long does it take?

We measure first. The new sealed unit is made to order, which usually takes a few days, and installation is typically under an hour per window.

Is foggy glass covered by warranty?

Many manufacturers warranty sealed units for 10 to 20 years. Look for a date or code printed on the metal spacer between the panes and send us a photo. We'll help you check.

Will the new glass match my other windows?

Yes. We match the thickness, tint and grille pattern so the window looks the same as the others.
