Skylight problems we fix

  • Foggy glass from a failed seal
  • Cracked or broken skylight glass
  • Leaks around the glass
  • Condensation dripping from the skylight in winter

Glass or the whole skylight?

If the frame and flashing are sound, replacing the glass unit and seals is much faster and cheaper than a new skylight. If water is coming in around the frame or through the roof, we'll tell you what's needed.

Skylight glass must be safety glass. We use the right laminated or tempered glass for overhead use.

Leak or condensation?

A skylight that drips in winter isn't always leaking. Warm, humid air rising to the cold glass condenses and drips down. If the water shows up only in cold weather and the glass is wet on the inside, it's likely condensation. If it shows up after rain or snow melt, it's a leak.

Winter ice dams

Ice that builds up on the roof around a skylight can push melting water under the flashing and seals. If your skylight leaks only when there's ice on the roof, the flashing or the roof around it needs attention, and we'll tell you if that's a job for a roofer.

Foggy skylight glass

Fog between the panes means the seal has failed, just like a regular window. We replace the glass unit and keep the frame when it's in good shape.

Common questions

Is a foggy skylight leaking?

Not necessarily. Fog between the panes means the glass seal has failed. A new sealed unit fixes it.

Why does my skylight drip only in winter?

Usually condensation: warm indoor air hitting cold glass. Ice dams on the roof can also cause winter leaks.

Can you replace only the skylight glass?

Often yes. If the frame and flashing are sound, we replace just the glass unit.
