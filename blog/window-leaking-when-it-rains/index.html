Window leaking when it rains? How to find where the water gets in

Water around a window after rain can come from the window, the caulking, blocked drain holes or the wall above. How to tell, and what fixes it.

Water on the sill or a stain under the window after a storm is a sign to act fast. Water in the wall leads to rot and mould. Here's how to narrow down where it's coming from.

1. Is it rain or condensation?

If the water appears in cold weather and the glass is wet on the inside, it's condensation from humid indoor air. If it appears during or after rain, it's a leak.

2. Look at the bottom of the frame

Most windows have small drain holes (weep holes) at the bottom. If they're blocked with dirt or old caulking, rain fills the track and overflows inside. Clearing them often fixes the leak.

3. Check the caulking outside

Cracked, shrunken or missing caulking between the window frame and the brick or siding lets wind-driven rain in. See window caulking.

4. Check the seals on the window itself

Worn weatherstripping on the sash, or a window that doesn't close tight, lets water through the window rather than around it.

5. Look above the window

If water shows up at the top of the window, it may be coming from the wall, the roof or the flashing above. That's not a window repair, and we'll tell you if you need a roofer or a siding contractor.

Basement windows

A window well that doesn't drain fills with water and pushes it through the window. See basement window repair.
