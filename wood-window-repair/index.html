What we repair

  • Soft or rotted wood in sills, sashes and frames
  • Cracked or missing glazing putty
  • Broken glass in wood sashes and storm windows
  • Sticking sashes and worn hardware

Repair or replace?

Small areas of rot can be cut out and repaired. When a sill or sash is badly rotted, we can replace that piece rather than the whole window. If the frame is beyond saving, we'll tell you and quote a replacement.

Keep water out

Rot starts where water sits. After repairing, we seal and paint-ready the wood and check the caulking around the window so it doesn't come back.

Signs of wood rot

  • Soft or spongy wood on the sill or the bottom of the sash
  • Peeling or blistering paint that keeps coming back
  • Gaps between the glass and the wood, or missing glazing putty
  • Dark stains or mould at the corners

Century homes and heritage windows

Many Toronto homes have original wood windows that are worth keeping. We repair rotted sills and sash parts, replace glass and glazing, and get stuck sashes moving again. If your home is in a heritage district, check with the City before you replace windows, because changes may need approval.

Keep wood windows healthy

  • Keep the paint and caulking intact so water can't get into the wood.
  • Clear snow and water from sills.
  • Fix small soft spots early, before they spread.

Common questions

Can a rotted window sill be repaired?

Often, yes. Small areas can be repaired. Badly rotted sills can be replaced without replacing the whole window.

Can you replace the glass in a wood window?

Yes. We replace single pane glass and sealed units in wood sashes and reglaze them.

Do you repair windows in heritage homes?

Yes. We repair the existing wood windows rather than replace them where we can. Check with the City if your home is in a heritage district.
