Handles we replace

  • Folding and fixed crank handles on casement and awning windows
  • Lever handles on tilt and turn and European windows
  • Lock handles on sliding windows and patio doors
  • Locking handles with keys

Handle or operator?

If the handle spins freely on its shaft, the handle itself or the splined shaft is worn. If the handle turns but the window doesn't move, the gears in the crank operator are stripped. We check which one it is and replace only what's needed. See window crank repair.

Matching colours

Handles come in white, almond, bronze, black and other finishes. We match the colour and style of your other windows.

Which handle do you have?

  • Folding crank handles on casement and awning windows. They fold flat against the operator cover.
  • T-handles and knob handles on older crank operators.
  • Lever handles on tilt and turn windows, which also control the locking points.
  • FAPIM lever handles on many Toronto and Mississauga condo vents. See FAPIM window repair.

Why handles break

Most crank handles fit onto a splined shaft, a short metal post with fine teeth. When the teeth inside the handle wear down, the handle spins without turning the window. Handles also crack at the base, or the small set screw that holds them works loose. Lever handles wear inside, so they turn without locking.

Why "just buying a handle" often fails

Handles differ in spline size, length, offset and colour. A handle that looks right may not grip the shaft or may hit the window sill. Send us a photo of the handle and the operator, and we'll bring a match.

Common questions

My crank handle spins without opening the window. Is it the handle?

Sometimes. If the handle is loose on the shaft, it's the handle. If the shaft turns but the window doesn't move, it's the operator. We check and replace the right part.

Can I buy a handle myself?

You can, but many don't fit because of spline size and shape. A photo of yours lets us bring the right one.

My handle turns but the window doesn't move. What's broken?

Either the handle's teeth are worn or the gears inside the operator are stripped. We check both and replace only what's needed.
